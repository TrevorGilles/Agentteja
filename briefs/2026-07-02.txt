🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-07-02

1. BYD NEARS DECISION ON SECOND EUROPEAN MANUFACTURING PLANT
BYD is close to choosing the location for a second European production facility, with Spain and France emerging as the leading candidates for a brownfield investment. This follows the Chinese automaker's recent launch of trial production at its first European plant in Szeged, Hungary, which targets up to 300,000 vehicles per year. The expansion signals BYD's intent to entrench itself in Europe ahead of tightening EU rules on foreign EV investment.

2. FERRARI LUCE SELLS OUT IN CHINA WITHIN HOURS OF LAUNCH
Ferrari's first-ever fully electric vehicle, the Luce, sold all 88 units allocated to China almost immediately after going on sale on June 27. Priced at around 550,000 euros ($640,000), the 1,035-horsepower grand tourer generated massive demand despite controversy over its unconventional design. Full deliveries are expected to begin in the fourth quarter of 2026, and the instant sellout underscores strong appetite for ultra-luxury EVs in China.

3. TRUMP ADMINISTRATION OPENS USMCA RENEGOTIATION WITH TOUGHER AUTO RULES
The Trump administration formally launched USMCA renegotiation talks on July 1, pushing for stricter North American auto content requirements that could reshape supply chains for U.S., Canadian, and Mexican automakers. The move adds uncertainty for manufacturers who rely on cross-border parts flows under the existing agreement. Industry analysts warn the talks could drive up vehicle costs if content thresholds are significantly raised.

4. HYBRID SURGE LIFTS TOYOTA, HONDA, HYUNDAI AND KIA IN JUNE
A wave of hybrid demand powered strong June sales for Toyota, Honda, Hyundai, and Kia, while GM posted a slip and the broader U.S. market saw continued fragmentation. European data similarly showed a 4% rise in new-vehicle registrations for the month, with hybrids overtaking both pure EVs and traditional gasoline models as consumers' top choice. The trend reinforces that hybrids, not full battery-electric vehicles, are currently driving volume gains across most major markets.

5. THE $20,000 NEW CAR IS EFFECTIVELY EXTINCT IN THE U.S.
The most affordable new vehicle price point has shifted sharply upward, with the biggest concentration of new-car purchases now falling between $30,000 and $35,000. Budget models that once anchored the entry-level segment — including the Chevrolet Sonic, Ford Fiesta, Hyundai Accent, and Honda Fit — have all been discontinued. Automakers cite thin margins and the cost of modern safety and technology requirements as reasons for abandoning the sub-$20,000 segment.
