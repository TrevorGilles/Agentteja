🚗 Automotive News Brief — June 22, 2026

1. Tesla Faces EU Regulatory Pushback Over FSD Speed Limits
European regulators are pushing back against Tesla's Full Self-Driving system over how it handles speed limits, raising fresh questions about the feature's compliance in the region. The scrutiny adds to mounting regulatory friction for Tesla in Europe as it tries to expand FSD availability there. It signals tighter oversight ahead for autonomous and driver-assist features across the EU.

2. GM Drops Android Auto From Its EVs
General Motors confirmed it is removing Android Auto from its electric vehicles and plans to phase it out across its entire lineup. The move continues automakers' shift toward proprietary infotainment systems, following GM's earlier decision to ditch Apple CarPlay. It could reshape how drivers expect to connect smartphones in future GM vehicles.

3. Stellantis to Build Next-Gen Opel Astra and Corsa on STLA Platform
Stellantis announced its next-generation Opel Astra and Corsa will be built on the new STLA platform, with four additional new models planned through 2030. The move is part of Stellantis's broader platform consolidation strategy to cut costs and speed up electrification across its European brands. It underscores the industry-wide push toward shared EV-ready architectures.

4. Dutch Class Action Against Tesla Over FSD Promises Grows to 7,000 Participants
A Dutch collective legal claim accusing Tesla of breaking "Full Self-Driving" promises on Hardware 3 vehicles has reached nearly 7,000 verified participants, with law firm Kennedy Van der Laan backing formal action. The case adds to Tesla's legal exposure in Europe over FSD marketing claims. A ruling against Tesla could set a precedent for similar consumer claims across the EU.

5. Ford Warns US Needs 350,000+ New Auto Technicians by 2029
Ford says the US auto industry will need more than 350,000 new technicians by 2029 to keep up with rapid vehicle technology changes, including EVs and advanced software systems. The warning highlights a widening skills gap as vehicles grow more complex. Automakers and dealers may need to ramp up training programs to avoid service bottlenecks.
