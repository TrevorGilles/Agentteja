🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-08

🚙⚡ 1. Rivian R2 First Customer Deliveries Begin June 9
Rivian is set to hand over the first R2 electric midsize SUVs to customers tomorrow, June 9, 2026. The R2 packs up to 656 horsepower, accelerates 0-60 mph in 3.6 seconds, and offers up to 330 miles of range — all at a more accessible price point than Rivian's flagship models. The launch marks a critical moment for Rivian as it broadens its lineup to attract mainstream buyers.

🇨🇳🛃 2. Chinese EVs Could Enter U.S. Market Despite Tariffs, Analysts Say
Industry analysts suggest Chinese electric vehicles may reach American consumers within the next few years, even amid steep tariffs and fierce regulatory opposition. The combination of aggressive pricing and rapidly improving quality is seen as a long-term pressure point for U.S. and European automakers. The debate is intensifying as Chinese brands continue to dominate globally.

💰📈 3. Used-Vehicle Prices Rise for Fifth Straight Month — EVs Lead Gains
Wholesale used-vehicle prices climbed for a fifth consecutive month in May 2026, according to the Manheim Index, with electric vehicles posting the strongest gains as elevated gas prices push demand. Nearly one in five new-vehicle loans now tops $1,000 per month, an all-time quarterly high per Experian. The data signals continued strain on car buyers as both new and used markets remain expensive.

🏎️🔋 4. Range Rover Sport Electric Details Coming at JLR Investor Day June 17
Jaguar Land Rover is expected to unveil full specifications for the Range Rover Sport Electric at its Investor Day on June 17, 2026. The reveal is highly anticipated as JLR attempts to electrify its most popular model line while maintaining its luxury off-road identity. The announcement could reshape the premium electric SUV segment.

🏪🔄 5. Dealership Consolidation Continues — Lithia Sells Ferrari Store, AutoNation Exits Ford Outlet
Major dealer groups are reshuffling their portfolios, with Lithia Motors selling its Ferrari dealership and AutoNation offloading a Ford store among deals tracked across eight U.S. states. The consolidation trend reflects ongoing pressure on margins and a strategic shift toward higher-volume or higher-margin brands. Industry watchers see this as a sign of broader restructuring in the franchised dealer model.
