🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-20

1. Stellantis, Wayve and Uber Team Up to Scale Robotaxis Globally
Stellantis, UK self-driving startup Wayve, and Uber announced a non-binding partnership to develop and deploy Level 4 autonomous robotaxis worldwide. Stellantis will build the L4-ready vehicle platforms, Wayve supplies the AI driving software, and Uber handles deployment through its ride-hailing network, with plans to reach cities across Europe and North America.

2. Ford Recalls Over 390,000 Vehicles Across Three Separate Issues
Ford issued recalls covering roughly 91,000 F-150s with daytime running lights that fail to dim, 255,000 Focus cars at risk of stalling from a prior faulty repair, and nearly 45,000 F-150s that may unexpectedly downshift into first gear. All three stem from botched earlier repair campaigns, prompting fresh dealer visits for affected owners.

3. Rivian Joins ChargeScape to Unlock Utility-Managed Charging
Rivian partnered with ChargeScape, the BMW-, Ford-, Honda- and Nissan-backed charging platform also used by Tesla and Stellantis, letting owners enroll in utility managed-charging programs through the Rivian app. Rivian's large battery packs make it the biggest single contributor to ChargeScape's grid-flexibility pool yet, letting drivers save money while supporting grid stability.

4. Ford's $30,000 Electric Pickup Spotted Ahead of 2027 Launch
Ford has been teasing its sub-$30,000 EV pickup, the first vehicle on its new Universal EV Platform, with test mules spotted publicly and a dedicated microsite tracking development. The truck will be built at a $2 billion-overhauled Louisville, Kentucky plant using large aluminum castings, targeting more interior space than a RAV4 and lower running costs than a Model Y.

5. Honda Opens Orders for Super-N Electric City Car
Honda began taking orders for its Super-N, a small zero-emission city car offering up to 199 miles of WLTP range and up to 94 hp. The launch adds to Honda's compact-EV push in Europe as automakers race to field affordable electric options against rising competition from Chinese brands.
