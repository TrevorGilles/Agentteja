🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-07-06

1. Honda CR-V Dethrones Ford F-150 as America's Best-Selling Vehicle
Through the first half of 2026, Honda's CR-V has overtaken the Ford F-150 as the top-selling vehicle in the United States. Honda's supply chain advantages and strong consumer demand for compact SUVs have driven the shift. The milestone marks the first time in decades a non-truck has led U.S. sales, signaling a notable change in American buying habits.

2. U.S. Auto Sales Rise 7.8% in June Driven by Hybrid Demand
American new vehicle sales climbed 7.8% in June 2026 compared to the prior year, boosted by rising gasoline prices pushing buyers toward hybrid electric vehicles. HEV models have seen some of the strongest gains, as consumers seek fuel economy without committing to full electrification. The results suggest automakers with robust hybrid lineups are well-positioned for the remainder of the year.

3. BYD Reports 403,000 NEV Sales in June, Up 5.5% Year-on-Year
Chinese automaker BYD recorded wholesale sales of 403,472 new energy vehicles in June 2026, a 5.46% increase compared to June 2025. Fellow Chinese EV maker Leapmotor posted even sharper growth, surging 94.5% year-on-year to 93,376 global deliveries in the same month. The figures underline continued momentum in China's EV market as domestic brands consolidate their lead.

4. CATL-Backed Battery Swap Network Hits 2,000 Stations Milestone
The Choco-SEB battery swap network, backed by battery giant CATL, deployed its 2,000th swapping station on June 30th, averaging over 200 new stations per month throughout 2026. The rapid rollout highlights the growing bet by Chinese EV stakeholders on battery-swap infrastructure as an alternative to conventional charging. The network's scale is increasingly attracting fleet and commercial vehicle operators looking for faster turnaround times.

5. GM's 2027 Chevrolet Bolt Faces Oversupply Despite Single Model Year Plan
General Motors confirmed the resurrected 2027 Chevrolet Bolt will be produced for just one model year, yet dealers are already sitting on 118 days of unsold inventory — nearly double the 60-day supply the industry considers healthy. The oversupply raises questions about whether demand for the reintroduced budget EV matches GM's earlier optimism. Analysts warn that steep incentives may be needed to clear lots before the model is retired again.
