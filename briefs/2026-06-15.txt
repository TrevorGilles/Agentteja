🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-15

1. STELLANTIS COMMITS €1 BILLION TO OPEL, CONFIRMS NEXT-GEN ASTRA AND CORSA
Stellantis announced the next-generation Opel Astra will be designed, engineered, and built in Russelsheim, Germany on the new STLA One platform, with the Corsa to follow. The euro 1 billion investment backs at least four new Opel models by 2030, with STLA One launching in 2027 to cut platform costs 20% while supporting affordable LFP batteries and 800-volt fast charging.

2. NISSAN RECALLS OVER 51,000 KICKS SUVS OVER BLANK DASHBOARD DEFECT
Nissan is recalling 51,598 model-year 2025-2026 Kicks SUVs after a software flaw in the instrument cluster can cause the display to go blank or show a blue screen during cold starts, hiding warning lights and safety alerts. Nissan logged seven technical reports and 205 warranty claims but no crashes or injuries; free software fixes begin with owner notices starting July 1.

3. HONDA RECALLS NEARLY 99,000 VEHICLES OVER FAULTY AIRBAG SENSOR
Honda is recalling 98,892 Honda and Acura vehicles from model years 2016-2026, including the Accord, CR-V, Civic and Acura MDX, after a front passenger seat weight sensor can crack and short-circuit, triggering airbag deployment even when a child or small adult is seated. Dealers will replace the sensor for free; owner notices go out by July 6.

4. FORD ISSUES "DO NOT DRIVE" RECALL FOR BRONCO SPORT AND MAVERICK
Ford told owners of 4,653 2021-2026 Bronco Sport and 2022-2026 Maverick vehicles to stop driving immediately after finding front lower control arm ball joints were improperly installed at the factory, risking separation and loss of vehicle control. Dealers are dispatching free mobile inspections or tows while a permanent fix is prepared.

5. JAPANESE AUTOMAKERS FACE NEARLY $40 BILLION HIT FROM U.S. TARIFFS AND EV POLICY SHIFTS
Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Mazda and Subaru absorbed roughly $27.6 billion in costs in the fiscal year ending March 2026 from U.S. tariffs and reversals in EV and emissions policy, with another $14.4 billion projected by March 2027. Toyota alone expects $17.2 billion in tariff costs over two years, pressuring automakers to rethink integrated North American supply chains.
