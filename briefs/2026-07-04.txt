🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-07-04

1. Honda CR-V Dethrones Ford F-150 as America's Best-Selling Vehicle
For the first half of 2026, Honda's CR-V has overtaken the Ford F-150 to become the top-selling vehicle in the United States, with the Toyota RAV4 also beaten out by supply advantages. This marks a historic shift as the F-150 has long dominated U.S. sales charts. The milestone reflects changing consumer preferences toward fuel-efficient crossovers and tightening truck supply.

2. BYD Poised to Overtake Tesla Again in Global EV Sales
Chinese automaker BYD delivered 557,090 fully battery-electric vehicles in Q2 2026, putting it ahead of Tesla's 480,126 deliveries in the same period. BYD has been expanding its international shipments aggressively, and the gap reflects its growing dominance in the global EV market. This is the second time BYD has reclaimed the top spot from Tesla after briefly ceding it earlier.

3. Tesla Posts Strongest Q2 Ever With 25 Percent Delivery Growth
Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2 2026, a 25 percent increase year-over-year and the company's best second quarter on record. The result ends two straight years of declining deliveries and signals a meaningful recovery for the EV maker. Analysts credit improved demand for the refreshed Model Y and expanded Supercharger access as key growth drivers.

4. DRAM Chip Shortage Squeezes Auto Suppliers
A global shortage of DRAM chips is hitting the automotive industry hard, with Tier 1 suppliers bearing the sharpest pain as vehicle electronics demand continues to outpace supply. Automakers increasingly rely on DRAM for infotainment, driver-assistance systems, and over-the-air update capabilities. Industry analysts warn the crunch could slow production schedules across multiple manufacturers through the second half of 2026.

5. Polestar Exits U.S. Market With Up to 25,000 Dollars Cash Back on Final Vehicles
Swedish EV brand Polestar is offering up to 25,000 dollars in cash back on remaining 2026 Polestar 4 crossovers as it prepares to withdraw from the U.S. market. The deep discounts reflect the company's effort to clear inventory before its exit. Polestar's departure underscores the increasingly difficult landscape for smaller EV brands competing against entrenched domestic and Chinese rivals.
