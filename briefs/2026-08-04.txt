🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-08-04

1. TESLA WEIGHS SALE OF CHINA BUSINESS AMID SPACEX MERGER TALKS
Tesla is reportedly considering selling its China operations as the company explores a potential merger with SpaceX. Separately, US auto safety regulators have opened a probe into 1.2 million Tesla vehicles over suspension failure concerns. The dual developments mark a turbulent period for the EV pioneer as it navigates both business restructuring and regulatory scrutiny.

2. BYD TARGETS TOYOTA AS NEXT RIVAL AFTER OUTSELLING FORD
Chinese EV giant BYD has declared Toyota its next target after surpassing Ford in global sales, aiming to become the world's top automaker within five years. BYD's strategy explicitly excludes reliance on the US market, banking instead on surging demand across Europe, Southeast Asia, and its home market. The company registered nearly 38,000 new vehicles in the UK alone in the first half of 2026, crossing the 100,000 cumulative milestone since its 2023 UK launch.

3. GLOBAL EV SALES HIT 9.6 MILLION UNITS AT MID-YEAR, UP 7%
Global electric vehicle sales reached 2.0 million units in June 2026, pushing the year-to-date total to 9.6 million — a 7% increase year-over-year. Tesla posted 480,126 deliveries in Q2, its best second quarter on record and first year-over-year delivery growth in two years. The figures signal a rebound for the EV sector after a sluggish start to the decade.

4. MERCEDES-BENZ REVEALS ALL-ELECTRIC GLA WITH 408-MILE RANGE
Mercedes-Benz unveiled the redesigned GLA with a fully electric variant offering up to 408 miles of WLTP range and 320 kW fast charging via an 800-volt architecture. A 48-volt mild hybrid option will also be available, with the new model slated to arrive in 2028. The reveal positions Mercedes to compete more aggressively in the premium compact SUV segment against rivals like the BMW iX1 and Audi Q4 e-tron.

5. OVERALL AUTO INDUSTRY SALES DOWN 2-3% AT HALF-YEAR MARK
Global vehicle sales volumes are running 2 to 3 percent below last year's pace through the first half of 2026, with major groups including Toyota (down 2.8%) and Hyundai (down 1.4%) reflecting the broader trend. Analysts point to persistent affordability pressures, higher interest rates, and slowing demand in key markets as contributing factors. Despite the dip, several manufacturers continue to launch new models and expand their EV lineups ahead of tightening emissions regulations.
