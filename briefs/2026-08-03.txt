🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-08-03

1. TOYOTA PRODUCTION DISRUPTIONS SPREAD FOLLOWING JAPAN EARTHQUAKE
Ongoing aftershocks from a recent earthquake in Japan continue to ripple through global automotive supply chains, with Toyota among the hardest-hit manufacturers. Production at multiple facilities has been disrupted, and the impact is now spreading to other automakers dependent on Japanese-made components. Industry analysts warn that parts shortages could persist for several weeks.

2. BYD EXPORTS SURGE 65 PERCENT IN 2026, EYES GLOBAL TOP SPOT
Chinese automaker BYD reported a roughly 65 percent rise in vehicle exports during the first five months of 2026, with particularly strong growth in Brazil, Australia, and the United Kingdom. Chairman Wang Chuanfu reiterated the company's ambition to become the world's largest automaker within five years. The milestone underscores the rapid globalisation of Chinese EV brands.

3. TESLA POSTS RECORD Q2 DELIVERIES AT 480,126 UNITS
Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in the second quarter of 2026, its strongest Q2 on record and the first year-over-year delivery growth in two years. The result signals renewed consumer demand for Tesla products after a difficult 2025. Investors reacted positively, viewing the figures as evidence that the brand's appeal has stabilised following earlier market-share concerns.

4. SLATE AUTO DEBUTS $24,950 ELECTRIC PICKUP, RIVIAN LAUNCHES R2
Slate Auto revealed a bare-bones electric pickup truck at a base price of $24,950, a price point unprecedented in the American EV market. Separately, Rivian officially launched the R2, its most affordable model yet, after exceeding its own production guidance. Both announcements signal that accessible electric trucks are no longer a distant promise but an arriving reality.

5. STELLANTIS IN ITALY TALKS; FORD AND STELLANTIS Q2 EARNINGS ASSESSED
Stellantis is negotiating expanded manufacturing partnerships in Italy, with discussions centred on Maserati production and a pipeline of affordable electric vehicles. Dozens of new models and broader industrial agreements are on the table as the company seeks to improve factory utilisation. Meanwhile, analysis of Ford and Stellantis second-quarter earnings shows mixed results for Detroit automakers heading into the second half of 2026.
