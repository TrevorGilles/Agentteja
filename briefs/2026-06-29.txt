🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-29

1. Polestar Forced Out of the US Market
The Trump administration's Commerce Department declined to authorize Polestar under the Connected Vehicle Rule over Chinese tech concerns, forcing the Geely-owned Swedish EV brand to halt new vehicle sales starting with model year 2027. Polestar dealers say they are "devastated," especially since one of the brand's models is built in South Carolina.

2. Toyota Recalls Nearly 21,000 EVs
Toyota issued a recall covering close to 21,000 electric vehicles, including models sold under its Lexus and Subaru brands. The recall adds to ongoing scrutiny of EV quality control as automakers scale up electric production.

3. VW Group Sells Majority Stake in Everllence to Bain Capital
Volkswagen Group sold a 51% stake in industrial engine maker Everllence to private equity firm Bain Capital, continuing what's being described as VW's broader financial fire sale of non-core assets. The move signals VW's push to refocus capital on its core automotive and EV business.

4. Honda CEO Survives Shareholder Vote Amid Earnings Crisis
Honda CEO Toshihiro Mibe survived a shareholder vote despite the company facing an earnings crisis, giving him a continued mandate to steer Honda through pricing pressure and EV transition challenges. The vote reflects investor patience but also growing scrutiny of leadership performance industry-wide.

5. UAW and Automakers Clash Over AI and Robotics
The United Auto Workers union and major automakers are sparring over the expanding role of AI and robotics on US factory floors, as automation accelerates across the industry. The dispute highlights tension between cost-cutting automation efforts and labor's push to protect jobs.
