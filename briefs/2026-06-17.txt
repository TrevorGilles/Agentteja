🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-17

1. Ferrari unveils its first electric car, the Luce
Ferrari presented the Luce, a four-door, five-seat EV co-designed with former Apple design chief Jony Ive's studio LoveFrom. It packs four motors for over 1,000 horsepower and 500+ km of range, priced at €550,000 with deliveries starting Q4 2026. The launch is notable because rivals Porsche and Lamborghini are scaling back EV plans amid weak demand, making Ferrari's bet a major industry signal.

2. Japanese automakers facing up to $40 billion hit from Trump policy whiplash
Toyota, Honda, Nissan, Mazda and Subaru have already absorbed roughly $28 billion in costs from US tariffs and EV-policy reversals, with the total projected to top $40 billion by 2027. Honda alone booked $9 billion in write-downs after canceling EV models, while Toyota expects a $9.5 billion hit to fiscal 2026 operating income. It underscores how unstable US trade and EV policy is reshaping manufacturers' product strategy.

3. GM says high gas prices are pushing buyers away from trucks and SUVs faster than expected
GM's North American president flagged that rising gasoline prices are accelerating a shift in consumer demand away from pickups and SUVs, the segments that generate the bulk of Detroit's profits. This matters because it could force GM and other automakers to rebalance production toward smaller or electrified vehicles sooner than planned.

4. Dealer confidence rises but outlook darkens, Cox Automotive index shows
Cox Automotive's Q2 2026 Dealer Sentiment Index shows dealer confidence climbing for a second straight quarter, even as expectations for coming months fell sharply. Rising costs, tight inventory and political/tariff uncertainty are cited as the main drags, signaling a bumpy stretch ahead for new and used vehicle sales.

5. EV battery supply chain projects in Canada mothballed amid sector slowdown
Several large EV battery plants in Canada have been paused or shelved even as the country continues courting future investment. The pullback highlights how softer-than-expected EV demand growth is rippling through the battery and raw-materials supply chain, not just automakers themselves.
