🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-09

1. TOYOTA CLOSES IN ON GM FOR TOP U.S. SALES SPOT
Toyota Motor Co. is on the verge of overtaking General Motors as the best-selling automaker in the United States for 2026. Through Q1, Toyota trails GM by fewer than 1,000 units while GM has shed roughly 67,000 units in sales year over year. The shift would mark a historic milestone and signals continued pressure on Detroit's traditional dominance.

2. MEXICO UNVEILS OLINIA — NATION'S FIRST HOMEGROWN ELECTRIC VEHICLE
President Claudia Sheinbaum unveiled Olinia, Mexico's first domestically developed electric vehicle, with a target launch price of under $9,000 when it goes on sale in 2027. The announcement positions Mexico as a new entrant in the global EV manufacturing race, aiming at affordability to drive mass adoption. The move is seen as a major step for Latin American auto industry ambitions.

3. TOYOTA CANCELS LEXUS LF-ZC AS JAPANESE AUTOMAKERS PULL BACK FROM EVS
Toyota has officially cancelled the Lexus LF-ZC flagship electric concept, the latest sign that Japanese automakers are scaling back ambitious EV timelines. Industry analysts link the retreat to sluggish consumer demand and fierce price competition from Chinese EV makers. The reversal raises questions about how quickly legacy automakers can pivot away from hybrids and internal combustion vehicles.

4. RECORD CAR LOAN PAYMENTS: NEARLY 1 IN 5 NEW-VEHICLE LOANS TOP $1,000/MONTH
Experian data shows that nearly 20 percent of new-vehicle loans in Q1 2026 exceeded $1,000 per month — an all-time quarterly high. Despite refinancing activity trimming an average of 2.2 percent off interest rates and saving consumers around $81 per month, affordability remains a major challenge for buyers. The trend points to continued strain on household budgets as vehicle prices and interest rates stay elevated.

5. EV CHARGING SECTOR SHIFTS FOCUS FROM GROWTH TO "INTELLIGENT PROFITABILITY"
Driivz released its 2026 State of EV Charging Network Operators Report, based on data from 300 senior professionals across North America and Europe, finding that the industry is moving away from rapid infrastructure expansion toward sustainable, data-driven revenue models. Operators are increasingly prioritizing efficiency, uptime reliability, and customer experience over raw charger count. The shift signals a maturing market as EV adoption accelerates and investor patience for unprofitable growth narrows.
