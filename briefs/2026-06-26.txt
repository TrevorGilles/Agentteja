🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-26

1. Trump Administration Bans Polestar From US Market
The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security denied Polestar authorization to sell vehicles in the US starting with the 2027 model year, citing the "Connected Vehicle Rule" that restricts Chinese software and hardware. Polestar, owned by China's Geely, will sell down existing Polestar 3 and 4 inventory and continue servicing customers, but dealers say they are "devastated" by the sudden loss of business. The move comes months after sibling brand Volvo, also Geely-owned, was granted US sales authorization.

2. Ferrari Replaces Marketing Chief After Luce EV Backlash
Ferrari named Massimiliano Di Silvestre, former President and CEO of BMW Italia, as its new Chief Marketing and Commercial Officer effective July 1, replacing 16-year veteran Enrico Galliera. The shakeup follows the rocky debut of the Jony Ive-designed Luce EV, which wiped roughly 8% off Ferrari's stock in a single day. It signals pressure on Ferrari's leadership as it balances electrification with brand exclusivity.

3. Toyota Cancels Lexus LF-ZC Flagship EV
Toyota has scrapped plans to bring the Lexus LF-ZC flagship electric sedan to production, though the next-generation battery and software technology developed for it remains ready for use elsewhere. A successor model is reportedly already in the works. The cancellation reflects broader industry caution as BEV demand growth slows and automakers reassess flagship EV bets.

4. Stellantis to Build Next-Gen Opel Astra and Corsa on New STLA Platform
Stellantis confirmed plans to produce the next-generation Opel Astra and Corsa on its new STLA platform, with four additional new models planned through 2030. The move is part of Stellantis' broader platform consolidation strategy to cut costs and speed up vehicle development across its many brands.

5. Tesla, Sunrun and Renew Home Launch 16-Gigawatt Virtual Power Plant
Tesla, Sunrun, and Renew Home announced a partnership to aggregate more than 16 gigawatts of home batteries, thermostats, and other connected devices into what they describe as the largest distributed power plant in the US. The project targets surging electricity demand driven largely by data centers, highlighting EV and home-energy companies' growing role in grid stability.
