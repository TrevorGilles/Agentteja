🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-28

1. Polestar Banned from US Market Starting 2027
The US Commerce Department denied Polestar authorization under the Connected Vehicle Rule, which restricts Chinese- and Russian-linked software and hardware in vehicles. Polestar will wind down US sales and marketing after the 2026 model year, while continuing to honor existing warranties. Notably, sibling brand Volvo received a waiver, highlighting the rule's case-by-case enforcement.

2. Volkswagen Weighs Up to 100,000 Job Cuts and Four Plant Closures
CEO Oliver Blume is reportedly planning the biggest overhaul in VW's 89-year history, targeting plants in Hanover, Zwickau, Emden, and Audi's Neckarsulm site, affecting over 45,000 workers. The move responds to weak demand, tariffs, Chinese competition, and costly EV transition expenses. A formal board discussion is scheduled for July 9.

3. Nissan Shareholders Revolt, Float Carlos Ghosn Return
At a raucous June 23 shareholder meeting, investors pushed a no-confidence motion against CEO Ivan Espinosa and rejected a board nominee. One shareholder even proposed reinstating fugitive former chairman Carlos Ghosn to the board. Though both moves failed, they underscore deep investor frustration with Nissan's turnaround progress.

4. BYD Sets 2030 Goal to Overtake Toyota
BYD Chairman Wang Chuanfu unveiled a target to become the world's top automaker by 2030, leaning on faster battery tech and global expansion. BYD ranked fifth globally in 2025 with 4.6 million sales, while Toyota sold more than double that and has held the top spot for six straight years, making the goal a steep climb.

5. Tesla to Ramp Giga Berlin Output to 7,500 Vehicles Weekly
Tesla plans to boost Grünheide production from 5,000 to 6,200 vehicles per week in July, then to 7,500 by October, adding 1,000 more jobs. The expansion comes as Tesla's EU registrations jumped over 150% year-over-year in May, signaling a rebound in European demand after a prolonged sales slump.
