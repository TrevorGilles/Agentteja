🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-27

1. Polestar Forced Out of the U.S. Market
The Commerce Department denied Polestar authorization under the Connected Vehicle Rule over its Chinese-linked technology, forcing the Geely-owned Swedish EV brand to halt new-vehicle sales in the U.S. starting with the 2027 model year. Dealers say they are "devastated" by the abrupt exit, which underscores escalating U.S. restrictions on Chinese-connected automotive tech.

2. BMW Slashes 2026 Profit Outlook
BMW sharply cut its 2026 guidance, lowering its automotive division's margin forecast to just 1%-3%. The move reflects mounting pressure from weak demand, pricing competition, and broader headwinds facing premium European automakers this year.

3. Mercedes-Benz Margins Squeezed by China Slowdown
Mercedes-Benz reported declining earnings as soft demand in China and intensifying competition pushed car margins down to 4.1%. The company says new model launches and tighter cost discipline are central to its planned recovery.

4. Senators Press NHTSA on Tesla's Full Self-Driving Claims
Two U.S. senators asked NHTSA to review Tesla's Full Self-Driving safety marketing after a Reuters investigation found its crash comparison statistics potentially misleading. The lawmakers are requesting stronger reporting standards and access to Tesla's underlying safety data.

5. Toyota Recalls Nearly 21,000 EVs
Toyota issued a recall covering close to 21,000 electric vehicles, including models sold under its Lexus and Subaru-linked lineups. The action adds to a string of EV quality issues automakers are managing as electric fleets scale up.
