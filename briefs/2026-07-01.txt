🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-07-01

1. Tesla Cybercab Hits Austin Public Roads as Robotaxi Deadline Expires
Tesla launched engineering tests of its purpose-built Cybercab on Austin public roads on June 30, sharing footage on X. The milestone arrives just as Tesla's self-imposed H1 2026 deadline to launch paid robotaxi service in five U.S. cities — Las Vegas, Tampa, Orlando, Miami, and Phoenix — expired without a commercial launch. Analysts are watching Q2 delivery numbers due July 2, with Morgan Stanley raising its estimate to 413K vehicles on strong European growth.

2. Toyota Closing In on GM as Top U.S. Automaker
Toyota is on track to report a nearly 1% rise in U.S. first-half sales to 1.25 million vehicles, while GM is projected to post a 7.2% decline — leaving fewer than 84,000 units between the two. Toyota's hybrid-focused strategy is winning over consumers, while GM and Ford, which bet heavily on full electric vehicles, are among the biggest losers in market share for the period. Cox Automotive forecasts a 16.1 million SAAR for June with Toyota, Hyundai, and Stellantis all gaining.

3. USMCA Review Opens Today, Auto Industry on Edge
The six-year review of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement officially begins July 1, forcing the Trump Administration to decide whether to extend the trade pact to 2036, let it expire, or withdraw entirely. The review is a flashpoint for the auto sector: USMCA provides 0% tariffs for qualifying North American vehicles, and any change would hit supply chains that have already been disrupted by 25% Section 232 tariffs on imported cars and parts. Automakers and suppliers are lobbying intensely ahead of formal renegotiation talks.

4. Tariff Wave Kills a Dozen U.S. EV Models in 2026
At least twelve electric vehicle models have been discontinued or paused for the U.S. market this year, driven by the removal of EV tax credits after September 2025, 25% import tariffs, and 100% duties on Chinese EVs. Hyundai, Volvo, Honda, BMW, Kia, and Tesla have all pulled specific models from American showrooms. U.S. EV sales are down 22.6% year-over-year in 2026, even as global EV sales are forecast to hit 22.7 million — highlighting a growing gap between the U.S. and the rest of the world.

5. Fourth of July Dealer Incentives Hit Multi-Year Highs
With inventory piling up, automakers are offering some of the largest consumer incentives in years ahead of the July 4 holiday. Kia is offering up to $16,300 in lease cash on 2026 EV9s, Chevrolet is cutting up to $9,000 off leftover 2025 Silverado trucks, and Honda is providing $14,000 in dealer cash on 2025 Prologue EVs. The deals reflect broader pressure from slowing sales, tariff-driven cost increases, and a consumer shift toward hybrids over full electrics.
