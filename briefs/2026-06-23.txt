🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-06-23

1. Ferrari's First EV, the Luce, Faces Mixed Reception
Ferrari officially unveiled the Luce, its first fully electric production car, developed with input from former Apple design chief Jony Ive. The four-door, five-seat model packs four motors for over 1,000 hp and 500+ km of range, but carries a $640,000 price tag and has drawn criticism over its styling.

2. Hyundai Recalls 421,000 Tucson and Santa Cruz Models Over Braking Glitch
Hyundai is recalling more than 421,000 2025-2026 Tucson and Santa Cruz vehicles after a front-camera software bug made the forward collision system overly sensitive, triggering unexpected braking. The defect has been linked to four rear-end crashes; owner notifications go out in mid-July.

3. Honda Recalls Nearly 99,000 Vehicles Over Airbag Risk
Honda is recalling almost 99,000 U.S. vehicles due to a seat weight sensor defect that could cause airbags to deploy unexpectedly in a crash, raising injury risk for occupants.

4. Nissan Recalls Over 51,000 Kicks SUVs for Blank Dashboard Defect
Nissan is recalling more than 51,000 Kicks SUVs after a software defect was found to cause dashboard displays to go dark, potentially hiding critical safety warnings from drivers.

5. China's NEV Boom Reshapes Global Auto Industry as Volkswagen Doubles Down
China's new energy vehicle sales hit 1.496 million units in May, up 14.4% year-on-year and now 56.9% of all new vehicle sales, cementing the country's lead in EV innovation. Volkswagen plans 13 new NEV models for China this year and more than 30 by 2029 as global automakers race to keep pace.
