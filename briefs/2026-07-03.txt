🚗 Automotive News Brief — 2026-07-03

1. TESLA POSTS STRONGEST Q2 EVER WITH 480,126 DELIVERIES
Tesla delivered 480,126 vehicles in Q2 2026, a 25% increase year-over-year and the company's best second quarter on record. The result marks Tesla's first year-over-year delivery growth after two consecutive years of decline. The rebound is driven by refreshed model demand and renewed consumer confidence in the brand.

2. TESLA LAUNCHES THREE-ROW MODEL Y L IN THE UNITED STATES
Tesla has introduced the Model Y L, a stretched three-row SUV version of its best-selling model, in the US and Puerto Rico. The Launch Series starts at $61,990 and is available for order online immediately. The move expands Tesla's family-vehicle lineup and targets buyers who previously had to look elsewhere for a larger electric SUV.

3. RIVIAN BEATS DELIVERY ESTIMATES IN SECOND QUARTER
Rivian produced 12,613 vehicles and delivered 12,194 in the quarter ending June 30, 2026, significantly exceeding its own guidance of roughly 9,000 produced and 11,000 delivered. The outperformance signals improving manufacturing efficiency at Rivian's Normal, Illinois plant. Investor confidence in the EV truck maker is expected to strengthen following the report.

4. EU AND US CLOSE IN ON AUTO TARIFF DEAL
The European Union and United States are finalizing a framework that would reduce the US tariff on European cars and parts from 27.5 percent to 15 percent. EU Trade Chief Maros Sefcovic said the European Commission aims to introduce the necessary reciprocal legislation by the end of August, with tariff relief potentially retroactive to August 1. The deal would provide significant relief to European automakers such as BMW, Mercedes-Benz, and Volkswagen, all of which have faced surging costs in the US market.

5. TOYOTA PROFIT DROPS 25% AS TARIFF COSTS REACH $8 BILLION
Toyota reported a 25% decline in net income during the first nine months of its fiscal 2026, directly attributing the damage to US import tariffs that cost the automaker approximately 1.2 trillion yen, or about $8 billion. The Japanese giant is lobbying for relief as US-Japan trade negotiations continue ahead of a July deadline. The figures underscore how deeply tariff policy is reshaping profitability across global automakers with large US exposure.
